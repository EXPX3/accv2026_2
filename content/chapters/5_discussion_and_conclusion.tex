\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support three main conclusions. First, frozen V-JEPA~2.1 features contain useful aerial geometry, but metric depth requires dense, scale-aware decoding. In Table~\ref{tab:mde_comparison}, the RGB and four-frame video linear probes reach 7.808~m and 7.226~m RMSE, whereas FiLM-DPT RGB achieves the best V-JEPA~2.1 standard-split result with 4.621~m RMSE and 0.129 AbsRel. This gap indicates that frozen transformer features are insufficient without reconstruction capacity and metadata. Because altitude and intrinsics determine the mapping from image evidence to metric rays, the FiLM pathway in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:metadata_vector}--\eqref{eq:film} is central to aerial depth estimation rather than an auxiliary refinement.

Second, video and altitude cues are helpful but conditional. DTA-FiLM-DPT Video is strongest at 30~m, reaching 2.939~m RMSE in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mde_comparison_by_altitude}, but FiLM-DPT RGB is stronger at 40~m and 50~m, with similar scene dependence in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mde_comparison_by_scene}. Thus, current V-JEPA~2.1 video tokens provide complementary cues but do not consistently outperform a well-conditioned RGB decoder. The altitude-interpolation result in Table~\ref{tab:occufly-depth-30-50} further supports physically meaningful conditioning: after training at 30~m and 50~m, FiLM-DPT RGB reaches 0.9653 $\delta_1$, 0.1021 AbsRel, and 4.7071~m RMSE at the held-out 40~m altitude. This result should be interpreted cautiously because scenes 01--08 appear across the interpolation protocol.

Third, improved depth helps SSC only when the depth prior, lifting module, and semantic context remain aligned. In Table~\ref{tab:ssc_overall_comparison}, replacing the DAV2 prior with the V-JEPA~2.1 FiLM-DPT RGB prior improves SSC mIoU in the CGFormer-style setting while preserving similar SC IoU. The best overall result is the FSSC RGB-context DPT variant with FiLM-DPT depth, reaching 37.68 SC IoU and 3.46 SSC mIoU. By contrast, conditioning both RGB context and depth reduces mIoU to 1.73, suggesting context--geometry mismatch. Pose-aware MVS shows similar sensitivity: FiLM-conditioning the MVS logits improves the unconditioned variant from 24.55 SC IoU / 0.17 SSC mIoU to 32.55 / 2.36, but remains below the best RGB-context configuration. The gap between SC IoU and SSC mIoU also shows that occupancy localization and semantic labeling remain distinct failure modes.

\subsection{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study isolates frozen-feature transfer and therefore does not test end-to-end finetuning. All depth heads operate from a $24\times24$ V-JEPA feature grid, video clips lack capture-FPS metadata, and SSC mIoU remains low in absolute terms. Overall, frozen V-JEPA~2.1 features provide useful aerial geometry for OccuFly, but strong aerial 3D perception requires DPT-style dense decoding, altitude/intrinsics conditioning, and lifting architectures whose depth and context pathways remain compatible.
