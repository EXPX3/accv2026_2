\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Monocular depth estimation and dense prediction decoders}
Monocular depth estimation is ambiguous because metric depth must be inferred from perspective, texture, object scale, layout, and learned priors. Early deep methods used coarse-to-fine CNNs and scale-aware objectives \cite{eigen2014depth}. Later work improved transfer through stronger encoders, multiscale dense decoders, and broader supervision. MiDaS showed the benefit of mixing heterogeneous datasets \cite{ranftl2020robust}; DPT reassembled transformer tokens into multiscale image-like features for dense prediction \cite{ranftl2021dpt}; ZoeDepth combined relative-depth generalization with metric specialization \cite{bhat2023zoedepth}; and Depth Anything/Depth Anything V2 scaled monocular depth training with large pseudo-labeled and synthetic data \cite{yang2024depthanything,yang2024depthanythingv2}. Aerial depth remains a distinct transfer problem because altitude changes scale, many images lack ground-level horizon cues, repetitive roofs and vegetation reduce texture, and far-range depth errors can degrade voxel lifting. We therefore compare frozen linear probes, DPT-style decoders, SFP adaptation inspired by ViTDet \cite{li2022vitdet}, and altitude/intrinsics FiLM conditioning \cite{perez2018film} on the same V-JEPA~2.1 features.

\subsection{Multiview stereo and cost-volume geometry}
Multiview stereo (MVS) estimates depth by matching calibrated observations across views. MVSNet constructs plane-sweep cost volumes, regularizes them with 3D convolutions, and infers depth from a distribution over depth hypotheses \cite{yao2018mvsnet}. Recurrent MVSNet reduces memory by processing hypotheses recurrently \cite{yao2019recurrentmvsnet}. This projective matching principle is directly relevant to UAV SSC: when poses are available, target/source correspondence can provide geometric evidence complementary to monocular priors. Our Pose-MVS variants adapt this idea to frozen V-JEPA~2.1 video features by predicting a categorical depth distribution aligned with FoundationSSC lifting bins, then using that distribution to place semantic context into the OccuFly voxel grid.

\subsection{Self-supervised visual foundation models}
Self-supervised visual foundation models learn transferable representations without task-specific labels. Masked autoencoders scale ViTs through high-ratio masked image reconstruction \cite{he2022mae}. I-JEPA predicts target-block representations from context representations in latent space \cite{assran2023ijepa}. DINOv2 produces general-purpose visual features from large-scale self-supervision \cite{oquab2024dinov2}, and DINOv3 emphasizes high-resolution dense features and transfer across natural and aerial imagery \cite{simeoni2025dinov3}. Video representation learning extends these ideas to temporal structure: V-JEPA predicts masked spatio-temporal features in latent space \cite{bardes2024vjepa}, V-JEPA~2 scales the approach to large image/video data \cite{assran2025vjepa2}, and V-JEPA~2.1 modifies the recipe toward dense image/video features \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21}. Probe3D and Lexicon3D establish frozen multi-backbone protocols for testing 3D information in visual representations \cite{elbanani2024probe3d,lexicon3d2024}. We follow this principle with matched V-JEPA~2.1 and DINOv3 heads and reserve backbone-specific claims for differences that survive this control.

\subsection{Camera conditioning and aerial benchmarks}
Camera-aware depth decoding predates our FiLM design: CAM-Convs inject calibrated camera parameters into depth networks \cite{facil2019camconvs}, while Metric3D canonicalizes camera geometry to improve metric transfer \cite{hu2024metric3dv2}. Our narrower question is whether altitude and intrinsics help decode metric depth from a frozen representation when decoder capacity is fixed. Aerial perception is also not limited to OccuFly. MidAir, WildUAV, UseGeo, AerialMetric, RuralScapes, Dronescapes, and MESSI cover complementary synthetic and real flights, geolocalization, metric geometry, rural scenes, and elevation changes \cite{fonder2019midair,wilduav2022,usegeo2023,aerialmetric2024,dronescapes2024,messi2024}. OccuFly is used because it jointly supports our standard-split MDE and voxel-SSC evaluation; cross-dataset real-flight transfer remains necessary future work.

\subsection{2D-to-3D lifting and camera-based semantic scene completion}
Semantic scene completion jointly predicts free/occupied voxels and occupied semantic labels. SSCNet introduced the task from single-view depth \cite{song2017sscnet}, and MonoScene lifted monocular RGB features into 3D \cite{cao2022monoscene}. Outdoor camera-based SSC then moved toward sparse voxel queries, deformable attention, and explicit view transformation. VoxFormer initializes visible voxel queries using estimated depth \cite{li2023voxformer}; TPVFormer represents 3D space with three orthogonal planes \cite{huang2023tpvformer}; and SurroundOcc/OpenOccupancy extend occupancy prediction to surround-view driving settings \cite{wei2023surroundocc,wang2023openoccupancy}.

Depth-aware lifting is a recurring theme. Lift-Splat-Shoot predicts a ray-wise depth distribution and splats image features into a shared spatial grid \cite{philion2020lift}; BEVDepth shows that explicit depth supervision improves camera-based BEV perception \cite{li2023bevdepth}. CGFormer addresses depth ambiguity with context-dependent voxel queries and 3D pixel-space deformable cross-attention \cite{yu2024cgformer}. FoundationSSC decouples semantic and geometric priors at both source and pathway levels before hybrid 3D fusion \cite{chen2025foundationssc}. Our SSC experiments follow this line but focus on aerial observations: V-JEPA~2.1 features provide semantic context, while monocular depth, video aggregation, and pose-aware MVS provide alternative geometry priors under a shared OccuFly protocol.
