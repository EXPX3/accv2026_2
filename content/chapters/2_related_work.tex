\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Aerial scene understanding and aerial 3D benchmarks}
Aerial perception has traditionally been studied through 2D semantic segmentation, object detection, mapping, and reconstruction.  UAVid \cite{lyu2020uavid} introduced high-resolution oblique UAV videos for semantic segmentation and highlighted challenges such as scale variation, moving objects, and temporal consistency.  Other aerial resources, including UrbanScene3D \cite{lin2022urbanscene3d}, support large-scale urban reconstruction and simulation from aerial observations.  These datasets are valuable for 2D and reconstruction-oriented aerial perception, but they do not directly provide the combination of RGB images, metric depth, camera calibration, and per-frame semantic occupancy grids needed to study aerial SSC.

OccuFly \cite{gross2026occufly} addresses this limitation by introducing a real-world aerial benchmark for camera-based 3D semantic scene completion.  It provides aerial RGB images, metric depth maps, semantic voxel grids, and camera metadata over multiple scenes, altitudes, environments, and seasons.  The dataset is especially relevant for UAV perception because it captures the geometric ambiguity of low-altitude flight: object scale depends on altitude, many surfaces are seen from oblique or partially top-down viewpoints, and free-space reasoning must be performed in a 3D volume rather than only on the image plane.  Our work uses OccuFly not merely as an evaluation dataset, but as a testbed for studying whether dense image/video foundation features and explicit multiview geometry transfer to aerial depth and semantic occupancy.


Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) is underconstrained because a single RGB image does not uniquely determine metric depth; the model must infer depth from perspective, texture, object size, shadows, scene layout, and learned priors. Eigen et al. showed that coarse global prediction combined with local refinement can learn single-image depth, while also highlighting scale ambiguity as a central challenge \cite{eigen2014depth}. MiDaS improved cross-dataset robustness by mixing heterogeneous depth datasets with compatible objectives \cite{ranftl2020midas}. DPT then replaced convolutional backbones with vision transformers for dense prediction, reassembling transformer tokens from multiple layers into image-like feature maps at different resolutions \cite{ranftl2021dpt}. More recent models such as ZoeDepth and Depth Anything/Depth Anything V2 combine large-scale pretraining, relative-depth generalization, metric-depth adaptation, synthetic data, and pseudo-labeled real data to improve zero-shot and metric depth performance \cite{bhat2023zoedepth,yang2024depthanything,yang2024depthanythingv2}. However, aerial MDE remains distinct because altitude, camera intrinsics, nadir/oblique view angle, and weak texture change the relationship between appearance and depth.

Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) jointly predicts volumetric occupancy and semantic labels, thereby combining geometry, free space, and semantic understanding in a single 3D representation. SSCNet introduced this formulation from a single depth image using a 3D convolutional architecture, showing that occupancy completion and semantic labeling are tightly coupled \cite{song2017sscnet}. Later work moved toward RGB-only and camera-based SSC. MonoScene formulated monocular RGB SSC by projecting 2D image features into 3D and using 3D context priors \cite{cao2022monoscene}. VoxFormer used depth estimation to initialize sparse visible voxel queries before dense voxel completion, making depth a key bridge between 2D images and 3D semantic volumes \cite{li2023voxformer}. TPVFormer represented 3D space using three orthogonal planes rather than a single BEV plane, while SurroundOcc and OpenOccupancy expanded occupancy prediction toward surrounding multi-camera autonomous-driving settings \cite{huang2023tpvformer,wei2023surroundocc,wang2023openoccupancy}. CGFormer further addressed depth ambiguity by using context-dependent voxel queries and geometry-aware lifting in 3D pixel space \cite{yu2024cgformer}. These works show that camera-based SSC depends strongly on how 2D features are lifted into 3D, but most are developed for indoor or automotive viewpoints rather than aerial UAV imagery.

Self-supervised visual foundation models aim to learn transferable representations without dense task labels.

In a posed MVS module, parallax becomes a measurable reprojection or feature-alignment error.
While MDE relies heavily on learned priors, Multi-View Stereo (MVS) estimates depth from correspondence across calibrated views. Learning-based MVS methods such as MVSNet construct a plane-sweep cost volume by warping source-view features into a reference view over multiple depth hypotheses using known camera intrinsics and relative poses; the correct depth should produce better feature alignment and therefore lower matching cost \cite{yao2018mvsnet}. R-MVSNet reduced the memory cost of full 3D cost-volume regularization by sequentially processing depth hypotheses, while CasMVSNet used a cascade strategy that narrows the depth range from coarse to fine stages \cite{yao2019recurrentmvsnet,gu2020casmvsnet}. DeepVideoMVS extended this idea to posed video streams, propagating geometry over time with recurrent spatio-temporal fusion \cite{duzceker2021deepvideomvs}. For aerial SSC, a pose-aware MVS frontend is attractive because neighboring UAV frames can provide motion parallax that is unavailable to a monocular depth head. Instead of using temporal video features only as generic sequence context, pose-aware MVS explicitly tests depth hypotheses through calibrated reprojection.

MAE showed that masked image reconstruction scales effectively for vision transformers \cite{he2022mae}. I-JEPA introduced a non-generative joint-embedding predictive architecture that predicts target-block representations from context representations rather than reconstructing pixels \cite{assran2023ijepa}. V-JEPA extended this predictive latent-space objective to video by predicting missing spatio-temporal regions in representation space rather than pixel space \cite{bardes2024vjepa}. V-JEPA 2 scaled the approach to internet-scale video and image data for understanding, prediction, and planning \cite{assran2025vjepa2}. V-JEPA 2.1 is particularly relevant for dense prediction because it explicitly targets dense visual representation learning through dense predictive loss, deep self-supervision across intermediate encoder layers, and unified image/video tokenization \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21}. These properties make V-JEPA 2.1 a strong candidate backbone for aerial MDE and SSC, but they do not automatically guarantee metric depth or volumetric occupancy accuracy; therefore, frozen probing, decoder ablations, metadata conditioning, and pose-aware MVS are necessary to test what geometry is actually encoded.

At the same time, modern visual foundation models such as V-JEPA 2.1 provide dense image/video representations that may encode spatial structure, semantics, and temporal continuity, but their transfer to aerial metric depth and semantic occupancy is not yet well understood. This work therefore studies V-JEPA 2.1 as a frozen image/video backbone for OccuFly, separating the effects of representation transfer, decoder structure, altitude/intrinsics conditioning, monocular depth priors, and explicit pose-aware multiview geometry.

Camera-based perception is attractive for these settings given its light weight, low cost, availability, and compatibility with UAV size, weight, and power constraints. This motivates methods that can infer geometry, semantics, and free space from visual observations, making Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) and Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) key components of UAV 3D perception that enable 3D scene understanding. Aerial imagery introduces altitude-dependent scale variation, oblique viewpoints, weak texture, and different occlusion patterns, demanding domain-specific solutions. Using OccuFly, a real-world aerial dataset with RGB images, metric depth maps, and semantic occupancy grids, we study whether V-JEPA 2.1 foundation features transfer to aerial MDE and SSC. Our ablations progress from V-JEPA 2.1 image-spatial \& video-spatiotemporal feature handling, from linear probing to DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer)-style multiscale decoding, FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)-based altitude \& intrinsics conditioning for MDE, followed by injecting conditioned depth-estimation priors, deploying a learnable UAV pose-aware multi-view stereo depth-estimation module, and transferring spatiotemporal features for SSC. Through this staged analysis, we separate the contributions of representation transfer, altitude conditioning, and depth-estimation variants to improving aerial SSC.

Earlier UAV datasets such as UAVid focused mainly on 2D semantic segmentation from high-resolution oblique UAV video and highlighted scale variation, moving objects, and temporal consistency as key challenges \cite{lyu2020uavid}. UrbanScene3D broadened aerial research toward large-scale urban reconstruction and simulation, but it was not designed primarily for per-frame semantic scene completion \cite{lin2022urbanscene3d}. OccuFly directly targets this gap by providing a real-world aerial SSC benchmark with RGB images, metric depth maps, semantic occupancy grids, camera metadata, multiple altitudes, and urban/industrial/rural scenes \cite{gross2026occufly}. This makes OccuFly a suitable testbed for studying whether visual foundation features transfer to aerial metric depth estimation and semantic scene completion.

\subsection{Monocular depth estimation and dense prediction decoders}
Monocular depth estimation is inherently ambiguous because metric depth must be inferred from perspective, texture, object size, scene layout, and learned priors.  Early deep approaches used coarse-to-fine convolutional networks and scale-aware objectives \cite{eigen2014depth}.  Later methods improved dense prediction through stronger encoders, multiscale decoders, and more diverse training data.  MiDaS demonstrated that mixing heterogeneous datasets with compatible objectives improves cross-dataset robustness \cite{ranftl2020robust}.  DPT replaced purely convolutional backbones with vision transformers and reassembled intermediate tokens into multiscale image-like features for dense prediction \cite{ranftl2021dpt}.  ZoeDepth combined relative-depth generalization with metric-depth specialization \cite{bhat2023zoedepth}, while Depth Anything and Depth Anything V2 scaled monocular depth training with large pseudo-labeled and synthetic data, producing robust general-purpose depth models \cite{yang2024depthanything,yang2024depthanythingv2}.

These models show that large-scale pretraining and dense decoders are crucial for depth, but aerial depth remains a distinct transfer problem.  UAV images often lack the ground-level cues used by indoor and automotive models: the horizon may be absent, object size priors vary with altitude, repetitive roofs and vegetation create weak texture, and far-range depth errors can dominate voxel lifting.  For this reason, our work does not treat a single depth model as a black-box solution.  Instead, we compare lightweight linear probes with DPT-style decoders on frozen V-JEPA 2.1 features, evaluate final-layer SFP adaptation inspired by ViTDet \cite{li2022vitdet}, and condition selected decoders with altitude and camera intrinsics through FiLM \cite{perez2018film}.  This lets us separate three factors: the geometric information already present in the representation, the spatial reconstruction capacity of the decoder, and the usefulness of explicit aerial metadata.

\subsection{Multiview stereo and cost-volume geometry}
Monocular depth relies heavily on learned priors, whereas multiview stereo (MVS) estimates depth by matching observations across calibrated views.  Learning-based MVS methods such as MVSNet build a plane-sweep cost volume by warping source-view features into a reference view over multiple depth hypotheses, regularize the cost volume with 3D convolutions, and infer depth from a probability distribution over planes \cite{yao2018mvsnet}.  Recurrent MVSNet reduces the memory footprint of high-resolution MVS by replacing full 3D cost-volume regularization with recurrent processing over depth hypotheses \cite{yao2019recurrentmvsnet}.  These methods are designed for depth reconstruction, but the underlying principle is directly relevant to aerial SSC: when camera poses are available, target/source correspondence can provide geometric evidence that is complementary to monocular appearance priors.

Our Pose-MVS variants adapt this cost-volume principle to frozen V-JEPA 2.1 video features and downstream semantic scene completion.  Rather than producing only a target-view depth map, the MVS branch produces a categorical depth distribution aligned with the FoundationSSC lifting bins.  The resulting distribution is used to place semantic context into the OccuFly voxel grid, making MVS a geometric frontend for SSC rather than a standalone reconstruction module.  This differs from the dense temporal attention depth experiments: temporal attention learns how to aggregate video tokens, while Pose-MVS explicitly projects source views into the target camera using intrinsics, relative pose, and metric depth hypotheses.  The Pose-MVS-FiLM variant further asks whether altitude and camera calibration, already useful for monocular DPT-FiLM depth, should also modulate multiview cost-volume logits before depth probabilities are formed.

\subsection{Self-supervised visual foundation models}
Self-supervised visual foundation models aim to learn transferable representations without task-specific labels.  Masked autoencoders showed that high-ratio masked image reconstruction can scale vision transformers effectively \cite{he2022mae}.  I-JEPA introduced a non-generative joint-embedding predictive architecture for images, predicting target-block representations from context representations instead of reconstructing pixels \cite{assran2023ijepa}.  DINOv2 demonstrated that carefully curated large-scale self-supervised training can yield robust all-purpose image features \cite{oquab2024dinov2}, and DINOv3 further emphasizes high-resolution dense features and broad transfer across natural and aerial imagery \cite{simeoni2025dinov3}.

Video representation learning extends these ideas to temporal structure.  V-JEPA 2.1 learns video representations by predicting masked spatio-temporal features in latent space and can be used as a frozen backbone with lightweight probes \cite{bardes2024vjepa}.  V-JEPA 2.1~2 scales this approach to internet-scale video and image data for understanding, prediction, and planning \cite{assran2025vjepa2}.  V-JEPA 2.1 further modifies the recipe toward dense image/video features through dense predictive loss, deep self-supervision, and multimodal tokenizers \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21}.  These properties make V-JEPA 2.1 attractive for aerial 3D perception, where one needs spatial layout, temporal consistency, and view-dependent geometry.  However, strong generic dense features do not guarantee metric depth accuracy or reliable 3D lifting under aerial viewpoints.  Our work therefore evaluates V-JEPA 2.1 through explicit downstream depth, cost-volume, and SSC probes rather than only through representation visualization or global recognition metrics.

\subsection{2D-to-3D lifting and camera-based semantic scene completion}
Semantic scene completion jointly estimates occupied and free voxels together with semantic labels.  SSCNet introduced the task using single-view depth input and 3D convolutional reasoning \cite{song2017sscnet}.  MonoScene extended SSC to monocular RGB by projecting 2D features into 3D and using 3D context priors \cite{cao2022monoscene}.  Camera-based outdoor SSC then evolved toward sparse voxel queries, deformable attention, and explicit view transformation.  VoxFormer uses estimated depth to initialize sparse visible voxel queries and then densifies them with transformer reasoning \cite{li2023voxformer}.  TPVFormer represents 3D space through three orthogonal planes, improving over a single BEV plane for semantic occupancy prediction \cite{huang2023tpvformer}.  SurroundOcc and OpenOccupancy expanded the problem toward surrounding multi-camera occupancy perception in autonomous driving \cite{wei2023surroundocc,wang2023openoccupancy}.

A recurring theme in these works is the importance of depth-aware lifting.  Lift-Splat-Shoot first formalized a camera-to-BEV representation by predicting a depth distribution for each image feature and splatting frustum features into a common spatial grid \cite{philion2020lift}.  BEVDepth later showed that explicit depth supervision improves camera-based BEV 3D detection \cite{li2023bevdepth}.  For SSC, CGFormer addresses depth ambiguity by generating context-dependent voxel queries and extending deformable cross-attention from 2D image space to 3D pixel space; it also combines voxel and tri-perspective-view representations to capture local and global 3D context \cite{yu2024cgformer}.  FoundationSSC argues that semantic and geometric priors should be decoupled at both source and pathway levels, using foundation features and geometric depth distributions before hybrid 3D fusion \cite{chen2025foundationssc}.

Our SSC design follows this line of work but differs in domain and experimental goal.  Existing methods are primarily optimized for indoor or autonomous-driving scenes, whereas we target aerial observations from OccuFly.  We use predicted monocular depth and depth probabilities as geometric priors for voxel lifting, while V-JEPA 2.1 features provide semantic context.  We then add Pose-MVS-Cost-Volume FoundationSSC to test whether calibrated target/source matching improves the depth distribution used for lifting, and Pose-MVS-FiLM to test whether altitude and intrinsics should condition that multiview distribution.  This allows us to compare three sources of geometry under a shared aerial SSC protocol: monocular depth priors, video-feature temporal aggregation, and explicit posed multiview correspondence.

\subsection{Position of this work}
The closest work to ours is OccuFly \cite{gross2026occufly}, which establishes the aerial SSC benchmark and reports baseline limitations of existing methods.  Our work is complementary: rather than proposing a new dataset, we use OccuFly to analyze the transfer behavior of a recent video/image foundation model for coupled aerial 3D tasks.  Compared with generic monocular depth models such as Depth Anything V2 \cite{yang2024depthanythingv2}, we focus on what can be recovered from frozen V-JEPA 2.1 features and how decoder structure, metadata conditioning, temporal context, and posed multiview matching affect metric depth.  Compared with camera-based SSC methods such as MonoScene, VoxFormer, CGFormer, and FoundationSSC \cite{cao2022monoscene,li2023voxformer,yu2024cgformer,chen2025foundationssc}, we explicitly study aerial depth priors and foundation features under UAV-specific viewpoint and scale shifts.  Compared with standard MVS methods such as MVSNet and R-MVSNet \cite{yao2018mvsnet,yao2019recurrentmvsnet}, our goal is not standalone depth reconstruction but semantic occupancy: the cost volume is used to produce the depth-probability tensor that drives FoundationSSC-style voxel lifting.  The resulting study connects four previously separate threads---aerial benchmarks, dense foundation representations, multiview cost-volume geometry, and depth-aware semantic scene completion---into a single evaluation of aerial 3D scene understanding.
