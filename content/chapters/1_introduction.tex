\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}


Aerial 3D scene understanding is essential for autonomous UAV operations such as cargo delivery, search-and-rescue, and human-UAV collaboration. Compared with ground robots or autonomous vehicles, UAVs operate under strict payload, energy, and compute constraints, making lightweight RGB cameras attractive for perception. IN the recent time mocular camera based scene understanding has gained significant attention. Especially in the groundlevel and indoor settings. However, aerial imagery introduces viewpoint and scale shifts that differ substantially from indoor and automotive settings demanding domain specific solutions.  
Earlier UAV datasets such as UAVid focused mainly on 2D semantic segmentation from high-resolution oblique UAV video and highlighted scale variation, moving objects, and temporal consistency as key challenges \cite{lyu2020uavid}. UrbanScene3D broadened aerial research toward large-scale urban reconstruction and simulation, but it was not designed primarily for per-frame semantic scene completion \cite{lin2022urbanscene3d}. OccuFly directly targets this gap by providing a real-world aerial SSC benchmark with RGB images, metric depth maps, semantic occupancy grids, camera metadata, multiple altitudes, and urban/industrial/rural scenes \cite{gross2026occufly}. This makes OccuFly a suitable testbed for studying whether visual foundation features transfer to aerial metric depth estimation and semantic scene completion.


Aerial three-dimensional scene understanding is a central capability for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in navigation, mapping, infrastructure inspection, search-and-rescue, human collaboration, and environmental monitoring.  In these settings, an autonomous platform must not only recognize visible image regions, but also infer metric geometry, free space, and the semantic occupancy of partially observed volumes.  Such a representation is important for collision avoidance, viewpoint planning, landing-zone assessment, and interaction with objects and humans in cluttered outdoor environments.  Camera-based perception is particularly attractive for UAVs because RGB cameras are lightweight, inexpensive, widely available, and compatible with strict size, weight, and power constraints.  However, inferring dense 3D structure from monocular or short video observations remains substantially harder than recognition, since the image formation process collapses scale, depth, and occlusion into a two-dimensional signal.  Aerial video offers an additional source of geometry through motion parallax, but exploiting it requires more than generic temporal feature aggregation: the system must relate target and source views through camera calibration, relative pose, and depth hypotheses.

Most progress in semantic scene completion (SSC) and camera-based 3D perception has been driven by indoor reconstruction and autonomous-driving benchmarks.  Early SSC formulations used depth as input to jointly predict volumetric occupancy and semantics \cite{song2017sscnet}.  Later monocular methods, such as MonoScene \cite{cao2022monoscene}, moved the problem toward RGB-only 2D-to-3D lifting, while VoxFormer \cite{li2023voxformer}, TPVFormer \cite{huang2023tpvformer}, SurroundOcc \cite{wei2023surroundocc}, OpenOccupancy \cite{wang2023openoccupancy}, CGFormer \cite{yu2024cgformer}, and FoundationSSC \cite{chen2025foundationssc} developed increasingly capable view-transformation, voxel-attention, and semantic-geometric fusion designs.  These works demonstrate that explicit 3D representations are valuable for scene-level reasoning, but their primary evaluation domains remain road scenes with ground-level perspective, relatively constrained camera height, and characteristic automotive object layouts.  Aerial viewpoints violate many of these assumptions: altitude changes the apparent scale of all objects, oblique views produce mixed top-down and side-view evidence, fine semantic boundaries can be weakly textured, and occlusions differ from those observed by ground vehicles.  Consequently, strong methods trained or designed for terrestrial scenarios do not necessarily transfer to UAV perception without careful adaptation.

OccuFly \cite{gross2026occufly} creates an opportunity to study this gap in a real-world aerial setting.  It provides RGB images, metric depth maps, camera calibration, and semantic occupancy grids for aerial scenes captured at multiple altitudes and across urban, industrial, and rural environments.  Unlike earlier UAV semantic segmentation datasets such as UAVid \cite{lyu2020uavid}, which focus on 2D dense labeling, or aerial reconstruction resources such as UrbanScene3D \cite{lin2022urbanscene3d}, OccuFly directly supports the joint study of metric depth estimation and 3D semantic scene completion from aerial camera observations.  This makes it possible to ask whether modern visual foundation representations provide transferable geometric and semantic priors for aerial 3D scene understanding, rather than only for image classification, 2D segmentation, or ground-level depth prediction.  It also enables a more precise question: when multiple calibrated UAV views are available, does explicit multiview correspondence provide a stronger geometric prior for SSC than a monocular depth estimator alone?

In this work we investigate V-JEPA 2.1 \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21} as a frozen foundation representation for aerial depth estimation and depth-aware semantic scene completion.  V-JEPA 2.1 and its successors learn by predicting representations in latent space rather than reconstructing pixels \cite{bardes2024vjepa,assran2025vjepa2}.  V-JEPA 2.1 further emphasizes dense predictive supervision, deep self-supervision at intermediate layers, and unified image/video tokenization, making it a natural candidate for dense 3D perception.  The central question of this paper is not whether a large foundation model performs well on generic vision tasks, but whether its spatial and spatio-temporal features encode the aerial geometry needed for metric depth and volumetric semantic completion.  We therefore keep the backbone frozen and vary the decoder, metadata conditioning, temporal aggregation, and geometric lifting mechanism.

We study this question along three axes.  First, for monocular depth estimation, we compare frozen linear probes against structured DPT-style decoders \cite{ranftl2021dpt}.  The linear probes test how linearly accessible metric depth is from V-JEPA 2.1 features, while the DPT decoders test whether multiscale token reassembly and convolutional fusion can recover fine spatial detail from frozen transformer features.  We also examine a Simple Feature Pyramid (SFP) adapter inspired by ViTDet \cite{li2022vitdet}, which converts a single final-layer feature map into a pyramid suitable for dense prediction.  Because aerial scale is strongly coupled to flight altitude and camera calibration, we further condition selected decoders with feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} using altitude and camera intrinsics.  Second, we compare spatial RGB features with spatio-temporal video features.  Aerial videos contain motion parallax and temporal continuity that may help resolve depth ambiguities, but such cues are not guaranteed to be preserved in frozen video representations.  We therefore contrast image-only features, repeated-frame controls, shuffled temporal controls, spatio-temporal linear probes, and dense temporal attention adapters.

Third, for SSC, we compare monocular and multiview geometric priors inside depth-aware lifting architectures inspired by Lift-Splat-Shoot \cite{philion2020lift}, CGFormer \cite{yu2024cgformer}, and FoundationSSC \cite{chen2025foundationssc}.  The monocular V-JEPA 2.1/FoundationSSC branch uses a DPT-FiLM depth-probability frontend to lift semantic context into the OccuFly voxel grid.  We further introduce a Pose-MVS-Cost-Volume FoundationSSC variant that replaces the monocular depth-probability frontend with a posed multiview stereo cost volume in the spirit of learned MVS cost-volume methods \cite{yao2018mvsnet}.  Given a target UAV view and neighboring calibrated source views, this branch matches target/source V-JEPA 2.1 video features across metric depth hypotheses, regularizes the resulting cost volume, and converts it into the depth distribution consumed by the FoundationSSC lifting module.  We also evaluate a Pose-MVS-FiLM variant that applies altitude/intrinsics FiLM conditioning directly to the MVS depth-bin logits before the depth softmax, so that the dense depth map and lifting probabilities remain consistent.

This design separates two different uses of video.  Dense temporal attention tests whether frozen V-JEPA 2.1 video tokens contain useful target-frame depth cues after nonlinear aggregation.  In contrast, Pose-MVS tests an explicitly geometric hypothesis: calibrated source views should constrain depth through projective correspondence, even when monocular appearance cues are ambiguous.  This distinction is important for aerial perception, where altitude variation, repeated textures, vegetation, roofs, and oblique viewing directions can make single-image depth uncertain while neighboring UAV frames may provide exploitable parallax.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We present a systematic study of frozen V-JEPA 2.1 spatial and spatio-temporal representations for aerial metric depth estimation on OccuFly.
    \item We compare representation probes of increasing structure: linear depth heads, DPT-style multiscale decoders, SFP-adapted final-layer decoders, and altitude/intrinsics-conditioned FiLM variants.
    \item We analyze the role of temporal evidence through video-feature probes and temporal-attention adapters, including controls that separate true temporal context from repeated or shuffled frame inputs.
    \item We extend the investigation from depth to semantic scene completion by incorporating predicted depth and depth distributions as geometric priors in LSS-, CGFormer-, and FoundationSSC-inspired aerial SSC variants.
    \item We introduce Pose-MVS-Cost-Volume FoundationSSC, which replaces monocular depth-probability lifting with pose-aware multiview cost-volume depth estimation from target/source UAV views.
    \item We further evaluate Pose-MVS-FiLM, a metadata-conditioned MVS variant that modulates cost-volume depth logits with altitude and camera intrinsics while preserving probability--depth consistency for voxel lifting.
    \item We identify the practical domain gap between ground-level camera-centric 3D perception and aerial 3D scene understanding, providing a focused evaluation protocol for future foundation-model transfer studies in UAV perception.
\end{itemize}
