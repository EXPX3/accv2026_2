\begin{abstract}
Aerial 3D scene understanding is central to UAV navigation, mapping, inspection, search-and-rescue, human collaboration, and environmental monitoring. Camera-based perception is attractive for these settings because RGB cameras are lightweight, low-cost, widely available, and compatible with UAV size, weight, and power constraints. However, most visual 3D perception research remains centered on indoor or ground-level autonomous-driving scenes, while aerial imagery introduces altitude-dependent scale variation, oblique viewpoints, weak texture, and different occlusion patterns. Using OccuFly, a real-world aerial dataset with RGB images, metric depth maps, and semantic occupancy grids, we study whether V-JEPA 2.1 foundation features transfer to UAV 3D geometric and contextual perception. We compare spatial RGB and spatio-temporal video features for monocular depth estimation and depth-aware semantic scene completion. Our ablations progress from linear probing to DPT-style multiscale decoding, Simple Feature Pyramid adaptation, FiLM-based altitude and intrinsics conditioning, LSS-style 2D-3D lifting, and FoundationSSC/CGFormer-inspired occupancy variants. We further study pose-aware multi-view depth cost volumes constructed from calibrated neighboring frames. Through this staged analysis, we separate representation transfer, geometric conditioning, and 2D-3D lifting, deriving practical design lessons for aerial depth and semantic occupancy prediction.

\end{abstract}
